When a recall hits, grocers absorb the blast.

FDA issues a recall. Your supplier notifies you eventually. Your customers have no idea. And when they figure it out — they come looking for answers. Here's what that looks like.

⚖️

Legal liability

Grocers have faced lawsuits and regulatory action for keeping recalled products on shelves — even days after FDA notice. ByHeart sent warning letters directly to Kroger, Jewel, and Walmart.

📰

Reputational damage

When a customer gets sick from something they bought at your store and learns there was a recall they never heard about, the story doesn't stay private. Local news loves it.

😡

Customer trust collapse

Trust takes years to build and one recall to break. Customers who feel abandoned during a food safety event don't come back — and they tell people.

📞

Operational chaos

Your staff finds out from customers, not the other way around. Phones ring. Product has to be pulled mid-shift. Floor teams are answering questions they don't have answers to.

📱

Social media exposure

"I bought this at [your store] and nobody told me." That post writes itself. And it gets shared before you've had a chance to respond or even confirm the recall.

🔬

Shrinking federal safety net

CDC's FoodNet was just cut from 8 pathogens to 2. Listeria is no longer federally tracked in community outbreaks. The warning system that used to catch things early is getting smaller.
